Project Background: RiceProTek

What is RiceProTek?

RiceProTek is an Alternate Wetting and Drying (AWD) irrigation decision-support platform for rice systems — turning raw soil-water sensor readings and weather forecasts into a clear, daily irrigate / hold / monitor recommendation for every field station.

🎯 Who it serves

👨‍🌾 Rice farmers & water masters
🏞️ Irrigators' associations (IAs)
🏛️ NIA / LGU agriculture offices
🔬 PhilRice / research partners

💡 What it delivers

📡 Live IoT water-level monitoring per station
🌦️ Weather-aware, season-adjusted irrigation triggers
🗺️ A single dashboard to prioritize where water goes next

Currently piloted for irrigation service areas in Midsayap, North Cotabato.

The Problem

💧 Manual irrigation decisions

Water masters historically decide "irrigate or wait?" using field visits and gut feel — expensive and inconsistent.

🌧️ Rain and sensor data live apart

IoT soil-water sensors report distance-to-water, but nobody merges that with rainfall forecasts to answer "will it rain before we need to irrigate?"

🌦️ Wet vs dry season needs differ

A single fixed irrigation trigger doesn't fit both the wet season (May–Oct) and dry season (Nov–Apr) in Mindanao rice systems.

🎯 Goal

Combine live sensor readings + live weather forecasts into one clear Irrigate / Hold / Monitor recommendation, per station, per day.

Soil Nutrient Monitoring: NPK

Alongside water level, the sensor platform also captures NPK values:

  • Nitrogen (N) — supports vegetative growth and leaf development.
  • Phosphorus (P) — supports root development and early tillering.
  • Potassium (K) — supports plant vigor, stress tolerance, and grain filling.

🌱 Agronomic meaning

NPK helps interpret whether crop performance is limited by nutrient status, not just water stress.

📊 Decision support

It complements water management by showing if the field is simultaneously facing water and nutrient imbalance.

The Science: Alternate Wetting and Drying (AWD)

Rice doesn't need to be flooded at all times. AWD lets the field dry down safely between irrigations, saving water without hurting yield.

  • Sensor reading = raw distance from an above-soil sensor down to the water/soil surface.
  • 20 cm raw reading is calibrated as "at the soil surface" (0 cm deficit).
  • Water level (55 cm ref) = 55 − raw distance, giving an intuitive "how full" reading.
  • Deficit beyond the irrigation trigger (season-dependent) ⇒ irrigation is needed.
🔵 Sufficient — at/above soil surface
🟢 Small drawdown — monitor
🟡 Watch — approaching trigger
🔴 Irrigation needed — beyond trigger

The Recommendation Engine

For every station, each day:

Projected change = drawdown trend
  + (ET weight × forecast ET0)
  − (rain weight × forecast rainfall)

This projects tomorrow's water distance from today's real sensor reading — not a guess.

Irrigate today Beyond trigger, no rain rescue expected

Hold for forecast rain Beyond trigger, but meaningful rain is coming

Schedule within 48 h Will cross trigger within 2 days

Monitor Comfortably below trigger

Dashboard: District Overview

  • 📈 Water-distance chart — observed history + projected forecast, irrigation-trigger line, rainfall strip
  • 📥 Full data tables with CSV download for every layer
